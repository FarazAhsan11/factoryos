# FactoryOS — modules

What is built, screen by screen, and the rules each one enforces. Moved out of
`CLAUDE.md` so it is read when you work on a module, not on every session; the
text is unchanged. For *how* a module is built and the patterns to copy, read
`IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE.md` and `IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE_2.md`. For the migration
behind a rule ("since `0033`…"), see `DATABASE.md`.

Auth, tenancy and factory setup are **built and working**; the first operational module has landed. Assume all of this is real before proposing to build it:

- **Auth** — login, password reset, invite → set-password flow, middleware session refresh, role-based routing.
- **Super-admin console** (`/admin`) — create / delete a factory (cascading wipe), branded email invites via nodemailer.
- **Factory workspace** (`/factory/[slug]`) — onboarding wizard gate, sidebar shell, dashboard.
- **The rail** (`FACTORY_NAV` in `src/lib/factory/nav.ts`) — sections **Operations / Quality / Assets & Maintenance / Improvement / Plant setup**, holding links and expandable **groups**. A page that used to have a tab strip (Pipeline, Shift log, Resources, Admin) has **no strip now**: each of its screens is a group child linking to `?tab=`, and the workspace reads the tab with `useSearchParams` + its `resolve…Tab()`. The rail switches between screens of the open page with `history.pushState` (no round trip, nothing remounted — half-filled forms survive). UI names differ from route names: Production planning → Customer orders (`/products`), Batch pipeline / *Room* schedule (`/pipeline`; the Batch families screen is unwired — its component and queries remain, with no rail link or tab); Operations → Production log / Shift report (`/log`), Log history (`/data`), Floor status (`/floor`); Issues & CAPA (`/actions`) is **unwired** — no rail link, the route and its code remain; Quality → Dashboard (Soon), Deviations, Non-conformances, Customer complaints and CAPA register (all `/deviations?tab=…`, with open counts beside them); Assets & Maintenance → Maintenance Requests (`/maintenance`), Preventive Maintenance schedule (Soon), Equipment register (`/resources?tab=equipment`); Kaizen ideas; Plant setup → Workforce (`/resources?tab=workforce`) and Configuration (`/admin`: Company profile, *Rooms*, Process stages, Departments, Shift pattern, Users). Italic names come from the factory's unit word. A new screen on a tab needs a rail child, not a strip; its tab-specific prefetch goes in `nav-prefetch.ts` keyed by the full href. Older mentions below of "tabs" / "Admin → X" / "the shift log's second tab" refer to these rail screens.
- **Admin & Settings** (`/factory/[slug]/admin`) — five working tabs: Company, Units, Processes, Departments, Shift times.
- **Resources** (`/factory/[slug]/resources`) — the two registers the rest of the plant names things from: Equipment (the machine register — number + name, `0028`) and **Workforce** (`?tab=workforce`, `factory_workers`, `0053`). Manager and up. Workforce is the people who perform and are assigned work — full name, department (from Configuration → Departments, optional), shift (Morning / Afternoon / Rotating) and a free-text role — with add, inline edit, delete (with Undo) and **Bulk add** (`WorkerBulkDialog`: paste rows from a spreadsheet or choose a CSV; `parseWorkers` in `worker-csv.ts` matches departments by name, refuses unknown ones and names already on the register, and fills blank cells from the dialog's defaults; the preview is what gets written). Nobody on it needs a login. Every "who" field picks from it through `WorkerSelect` (`src/components/factory/worker-select.tsx`): shift-log operators (`OperatorPicker`, the shift's own people first), maintenance *Work assigned to*, Issues & CAPA *Assign to* and *Who is looking into this?*, and the deviation case / CA / PA / CO owners. The stored value is the person's **name**, and "Not on the list…" types one for a contractor or cover. Left typed on purpose: signatures and statements — stage sign-off, deviation raised-by / supervisor / QA reviewer / closed-by, the shift supervisor in Shift pattern.
- **Users** (Configuration → Users, `/admin?tab=users`) — the logins: `profiles`, with email, access role, default shift and invite status (`UsersPanel`, invite + CSV import through the Server Actions in `employee-actions.ts`). It was Resources → Employees, when that one list was both the logins and the shift log's operators; `/resources?tab=employees` redirects here. Manager and up can see it; adding, inviting and removing still needs admin. An invite emails a link where the person sets their own password.
- **Products** (`/factory/[slug]/products`) — the batch catalogue, its own item in the rail directly under **Pipeline**: a batch is raised here, with its customer order, and planned there. It was Resources' third tab (and an Admin tab before that); `/resources?tab=products` redirects here. Manager and up, same `ProductsPanel` and queries — the components still live in `src/components/factory/admin/`. Each product shows a **status**: **Received** from the moment it is added, until the batch has a card on the board, then the card's own Planned / In production / On hold / Finished, in the board's colours. It is derived by `productStatus()` in `product-queries.ts` from `pipeline_jobs.status`, **never stored** — the shift log already moves the card, and a copy on the product would be a second record of the same fact to fall out of step. Split by two pills — **Open**, meaning not yet planned or still on the board, and **Finished**, meaning every pipeline job for the batch is signed off; read from `pipeline_jobs`, not from the row's own Active / Retired chip). Since `0041` a product also carries the **customer order** it is made against — customer code and name, SO order no, order value, rep, order received, due date, expected start and finish. **Add product** is a dialog beside Bulk import (the inline strip it replaced could not hold fifteen fields), and both it and the row's **Edit** dialog (`EditProductDialog`, the pencil in the last column, Actions) render one `ProductForm`. There is no read-only detail view: the table shows every field as its own column — batch, product, product code, work order, status, required qty, customer, customer code, SO order no, order value, rep, order received, exp. start, exp. finish, due, planned for, added, actions — scrolling sideways with only the batch number pinned. The customer name fills in from a code the catalogue has already seen. Bulk import reads the same columns, matching headers after `normalizeHeader` with each column claimed once (`resolveHeaders` in `csv.ts`), accepts `30-Sep-24` dates as well as ISO (`parseDateCell`), and names any header it didn't recognise in the review.- **Shift log** (`/factory/[slug]/log`) — the entry form, activity feed, and amendments. The form has **three shapes**, chosen by the `category` set on the activity in Admin → Processes (`0030`): **downtime** asks for room, activity, start, end, batch and comments and nothing else — no quantities, no operators, no action flag; **preparatory** (mixing, drying, granulation) adds one quantity plus the unit the room counts in — batches / kg / litres / drums / containers / units, stored in `qty_unit`; **production** is the full record as before — equipment and speed on a machine stage, a target derived from speed × duration, actual and rejected quantities. **Duration is net of scheduled breaks:** any minutes of the entry's Start → End that fall inside a break set in Admin → Shift times (either shift's, wrap-aware, overlaps counted once) come off `duration_minutes` and so off the derived target (`entryDuration` / `breakMinutesWithin`); the form shows how much was taken off. The shift is still resolved from the raw span. Entries stored before this keep the gross figure until corrected. `has_machine` and `has_output` survive as *derived* columns, no longer editable, so the pipeline trigger and every report keep reading what they always read. Two tabs and no page heading — the strip is the first thing on the screen: **Log entry** (the form, with the shift's activity feed beside it) and **Shift report**, which used to be its own nav item. Reading the sheet and correcting an entry are the same job done minutes apart, so they are one screen now; the report tab is supervisor and up (`logTabsFor()` in `log-tabs.ts`), and the heading and page width belong to the open tab rather than to the route. The entry pair is hidden by class, not unmounted, so a half-filled entry survives a look at the report. Log entry has a **Form view / Grid view** switch beside the tab strip (`?view=grid`, `LOG_VIEWS` in `log-tabs.ts`): the form is unchanged; the grid (`src/components/factory/log/grid/`) is every active room from Admin → Units, one editable line each, in room order. Each line is its own `useForm` over `logEntrySchema`, written through `createLogEntry` with the form's prefill (shift start clock, shift derived from times, product and target resolved, carry-over after a save) and the form's batch-gate wording — no second set of rules. Fields with no column (stage choice, slow reason) and every warning appear in a strip under the line only when needed; the speed unit is one combined select. There is **one button for the whole sheet**, at the bottom: **Log** when one line is waiting, **Log all (N)** when more are. Each room opens with a **heading band** — its name, and "Nothing logged today." when that is so — as the shift report's rooms do, and closes with **+ Add row** under its last row. **A room has no line until + is pressed** (as many as needed, each removable); a new line opens blank but for its Start, which is the end typed on the room's line above it, else the end of the room's last entry filed today, else empty. Cells that fill themselves in — Hrs, Product, Product code, Unit on a production line, Shift target — are shaded indigo (`Static`), from the moment the line opens. A line counts as waiting once it has an activity or batch; it says **Not logged** in its last cell until filed. Log all validates every waiting line first, then files the valid ones **one at a time in room order** (never in parallel — the database judges each entry against the ones before it); an incomplete line stays put with its errors in its strip, and a line the database refuses shows the reason there. A filed line is removed — its entry now shows above, locked — so the next + starts where it ended. **Every entry filed today** — from either view, by anyone — shows as a locked teal row above its room's line (`logKeys.day`, the activity feed's query), so the sheet shows what is done and what is left; corrections stay in the feed and the shift report. **Clear history** in the grid's header takes those rows off the sheet for whoever pressed it, for the rest of the working day (`log-grid-cleared.ts`, entry ids in localStorage, per factory and user). Nothing is deleted — the entries stay in the feed, the shift report and Log history — typed lines stay put, an entry filed afterwards shows as usual, a new line's Start still follows the room's last entry, and a cleared room's band says "History cleared." rather than "Nothing logged today.". **Unlogged lines survive a reload**: they are kept in localStorage (`log-grid-draft.ts`, per factory and user, for the working day only) and removed the moment they are filed or cleared — unlike the form's draft, the grid's keeps the quantity, because a line is restored as the very entry it was. Columns widen by dragging a heading's edge, up to the longest value in the column (`useColumnResize`, shared with the shift report). The grid puts its columns in the **Log history's order** — Start → End and run time first, then room, activity, batch, product, then quantities, speeds, equipment and operators, then flag and comments (`GRID_COLUMNS` in `grid-cells.tsx`); its Start → End cell holds both clock pickers. The shift report is laid out as the plant's own sheet, one flat row per entry with the room on every row, in seven groups: **Entry** (Room, Activity / Stage), **Time** (Start → End, Duration), **Product** (Batch, Product, Product Code), **Output** (Qty produced, Unit), **Against target** (Shift target, Rejected, Accumulative Total), **Machine** (Target speed, Actual speed, Slow reason, EQ No.) and **Team & notes** (Operators, Comments). Each group is a panel: a titled, colour-capped heading, and a gap either side rather than a rule. Calculated columns carry a pale indigo tint. The action flag sits under the activity and progress under the accumulative total; a production entry's Unit is its stage's `target_unit`. The arrangement lives in `REPORT_GROUPS` and `REPORT_COLUMNS` (`report/report-columns.ts`) and the CSV follows its order, with Flag, Progress % and Speed unit after. Above the table the masthead is one slim bar — shift, date and the five figures — with the factory, shift window and supervisor folded under **Details**: closed by default, remembered per browser, and always open on paper. The shift report has **no Add entry** — entries are filed from the log form or the grid; the report reads them and corrects them (`new-entry-row.tsx` is unwired and kept for now). It lists **only what was logged**: a room with no entries is not on the sheet, in the CSV or on paper, and a day and shift with none says "Nothing logged on 31 Oct 2026" under the shift's name. An entry that has been amended carries a violet history icon beside its pencil, and only an amended entry does; it opens the entry's **amendment history** — Date, Time, What was amended, Reason for amendment, Amended by — for anyone who can read the sheet. The rows come from `shift_log_amendments` (`0055`), written by a trigger and read-only to the app; an amendment from before `0055` shows "Not recorded" for what the old record never kept. The grid mounts on first use and is then hidden by class, like the form.
- **Data table** (`/factory/[slug]/data`) — server-side filter / sort / paginate over the shift log, CSV export.
- **Shift report** — one day, one shift, every room on one sheet. Rooms that logged nothing are *kept*, showing their pipeline status; it prints as a landscape handover document. Supervisor and up. It lives in the **shift log's second tab** (`/factory/[slug]/log?tab=report`); `/factory/[slug]/report` is now a redirect kept for old links and printed sheets.
- **Kaizen** (`/factory/[slug]/kaizen`) — improvement ideas from the floor and the queue that reviews them, under **Analytics** where Shift report used to be. It was the shift log's second tab, which put it behind a screen someone opens with a half-filled entry in front of them. Anyone may raise an idea; review is supervisor and up (`can_review_factory()`, `0019`). Components live in `src/components/factory/kaizen/`; the schemas stayed in the log's `schemas.ts`.
- **Pipeline** (`/factory/[slug]/pipeline`) — the batch board, plus **Batch families** and per-batch **stage planning**. The board is a **list, one row per batch** (`pipeline-board.tsx`; it was four Kanban columns): status as a coloured spine and chip, filtered by status and type, the arrow expanding a row to its stage strip and — for a bulk batch — its finished lots folded underneath; the name opens `JobDetailDialog`, **Stages** the plan, **+ Lot** New batch preset to that parent (the bulk is read back at the top and the dialog is its lots — as many as added, each with its own plan — the same cards New batch shows beneath a Bulk Production). Statuses move themselves from the shift log via a database trigger; nobody sets one by hand. Three ways in: **New batch** (a two-step dialog — pick manufacturing / packing / combined, then the batch itself *from the catalogue*, never typed — which can also write the batch's **stage plan** as a table (`StagePlanEditor`, `stagePlanSchema`) and **issue** it, and on Bulk Production raise one or more paired Finished Lots, each with its own plan (the lots are added up against the bulk, advisory only); every part is validated before anything is saved, and a failure part way rolls back every job it created), **From catalogue** for adding several at once, or a `planned_for` date on the batch in Admin → Products, promoted onto the board when the page loads on or after that day (`promote_scheduled_jobs()`, migration `0018`). Since `0031` a job carries a **type** — stored as `manufacturing | packing | combined`, which is what the database's check and every guard read, but labelled **Bulk Production / Finished Lot / Single Batch** in the UI from `BATCH_TYPES` in the pipeline schemas; renaming a label is wording, renaming a value is a migration. A single batch carries both halves under one "Single batch details" block, in New batch and Edit batch alike — the bulk's (bulk unit, overage) and the lot's (pack size, pack unit, market) — because it is made and packed under one number; `createBatchJob` / `updateBatchJob` save `bulk_unit` and `market` for it too. Never a parent or a bulk received: the 0031 family guard refuses a parent on anything but packing, and `bulk_consumed` is gated on `batch_type = 'packing'` so it draws no allocation panel for bulk nobody gave it. Its **ordered quantity is shown, never typed**: it is `factory_products.required_qty`, and a second copy on the job would give the overrun check and the plan different numbers. When it is a packing run a job also carries a **parent** — the manufacturing batch whose bulk it draws on. That is what the Batch families tab reads: parent 46000 with children 46001/2/3 underneath, and a bar comparing Σ (children's containers × pack size) against the parent's bulk target. Over-allocation is badged, never blocked.

  The **Schedule** tab (`?tab=schedule`) is the plan read by room instead of by batch — the one arrangement the module could not produce, and the question a planner in front of the board actually has. Its two views and the **Gantt timeline** tab (`?tab=gantt`) are three views over one derivation (`buildRoomLanes` in `src/lib/factory/schedule.ts`), so they cannot disagree. The Schedule's **Board** (`schedule-board.tsx`) is the glance: one row per room, three stages deep (current / next up, then, following), with `+N more` handing a longer room to the Queue. The **Gantt timeline** is a calendar (`schedule-calendar.tsx`, 2 / 4 / 8 weeks, drag a stage to move it, holidays and weekly days off tinted) — rooms down the side in name order, a fortnight across from this week's Monday (Prev / Today / Next move it a week), each stage a bar over the days `stageSpan` gives it: planned date to estimated finish, one day without an estimate, and a running stage at least to today (undated, it is drawn today). Solid is running, a tint is planned, and the colour is hashed from the batch so one batch reads the same in every room. **Queue** is the work surface, every stage in every room with its dates. Overlapping stages in one room are **stacked**, never drawn over each other; a day with two or more from today on is a **clash** — tinted red, the bars ringed, the room labelled "Clash · N days" — and `findRoomConflicts` (one per run of days with the same stages, from today, a year ahead) feeds a banner above the calendar whose **Show** moves the window to the clash and highlights the room. Past three stacked tracks a day says `+N more`, and a room's undated stages are a chip; both hand the room to the Room schedule's Queue (`?tab=schedule&room=<id>`), scrolled to it and ringed. A room with no work in the window offers a jump to its next (or last) dated stage. A room with nothing left to run is absent from both — a lane of dashes tells a planner nothing and twenty-five of them buries the four rooms that matter — and a stage with no room is counted and named rather than silently dropped. A running stage heads its lane whatever the calendar says. Clicking any card or batch number opens `StageEditDialog` (the whole stage, one form, including whether the room needs a **Full Clean (FC)** or **Provisional Clean (PC)** after it and for how many hours — `0052` — drawn as a hatched FC / PC segment at the end of the bar, sized by hours against the bar's days); the Queue's **Est. finish** and **comment** are edited in place instead, because a planner sets twenty of them in a sitting and a dialog per number turns that into five minutes. Both read the stages and jobs the workspace already holds, so the tab costs no extra request.

  Since `0033` each batch also carries a **plan**: one row per stage that produces something (Dispensing 500 kg → Compression 210,000 tablets → Coating → Packing), each with its own target and unit. The **last stage in the plan completes the order** — derived from position, so reordering moves it — and signing it off is what moves the card to Finished. Since `0038` the shift log accepts a `production` entry only when four things hold: the typed batch number names a product, that product has a card on the board, the card is **issued for production** (issuing is itself refused until every stage has a target), and the activity is a stage in that batch's plan. Since `0054` a `preparatory` entry needs only the first two — dispensing and mixing start while the plan is still being drawn up — and takes a stage only once the batch is issued and its plan has one for that activity; before issue it is filed against the batch with no stage, so it is on the batch record and the shift report but not in a stage's progress bar. A card stays in Planning until it is issued, whatever is logged against it. Downtime is exempt from the last two — setup delays happen before issue, and no downtime activity is plannable — and needs no batch at all; but a number it *does* name must be real and on the board. The log resolves an entry to its stage automatically, and asks *which* only where a batch runs one activity several times (three packing runs, or three work orders under one number).

  Since `0037` each batch also carries a **tolerance** — a percentage, set on the New batch form beside priority and the due date, set once on the batch and inherited by every stage in its plan, never set per stage (the column allows an override; the app does not write one). It is the ceiling the shift log enforces: a stage planned for 20 kg at 5% accepts up to 21 kg, and an entry that would take it past that is **refused**, with the numbers that explain why. The stage's progress card in the log turns red as the quantity is typed, before the submit, and the plan's own bar and the card's stage strip go red for a stage left over the line by a tightened plan. Not the same knob as overage: that is extra deliberately made against the work order and only raises a flag.
- **Issues & CAPAs** (`/factory/[slug]/actions`) — **unwired from the rail**: the route, components, queries and database rules all remain, and flagged shift entries still raise issues (seen on the Batch record's Issues tab and Floor status counts), but no rail link leads to the screen. The accountability loop. A flagged shift entry raises an issue, which then works through five stages — **Open → Investigating → Action taken → Verification → Closed**, one at a time, each move paid for with the evidence that stage produces, and since `0027` each stage collecting the thing it is *named after*: an owner, then a root cause, then the corrective (+ preventive) action, then the sign-off. Enforced by the `actions_stage_transition` trigger, not the UI. One legal skip: an **open** issue can be resolved straight to Closed by a supervisor when it needs no CAPA — it costs a written account of what was done (stored in `verification`) and leaves `investigating_at` null, which is what `resolved_direct` reads to label the text a *resolution* and draw the three middle stages as skipped (`0026`). Overdue and escalated are *computed*, never stored, and there are two clocks: the fix clock runs through Open, Investigating and Action taken, stopping once the corrective action is in, and a slower sign-off clock (`factories.escalate_hours`) runs through Verification.
- **Maintenance** (`/factory/[slug]/maintenance`) — the Breakdown Maintenance Request, all three sections of it. Report a fault against an equipment number (resolved to a name from the register), name the *initiating* department and the department *needed*, type the affected batch; requests are numbered `MR-2026-014` by a counter table. Opening one opens the document as **three tabs** — **Initiation → Engineering → QA review** — and the five statuses walk it: `reported → assigned → in_progress → completed → verified`, one at a time, forward only, each move paid for with what that section records. A name and a department to assign; nothing to start work (but that stamp is what makes downtime mean the machine rather than the paperwork); the work record plus the cleaning and production-review answers to complete; the change-control and deviation answers plus a signature to verify. Both yes/no pairs are tri-state — null is *unanswered* and the gate refuses it, because an unanswered box that prints as "No" is what the paper form gets wrong. Enforced by `maintenance_stage_transition` (`0029`), not the UI; supervisor and up may drive it, anyone may raise one. Response and downtime are computed in the view, never stored.
- **Quality** (`/factory/[slug]/deviations`) — since `0057` one page with four screens chosen in the rail by `?tab=` (`quality-tabs.ts`): **Deviations** (`deviations` — planned and unplanned, with a Planned / Unplanned chip), **Non-conformances** (`ncr`), **Customer complaints** (`complaints`) and the **CAPA register** (`capa`); **Dashboard** is listed as Soon and `?tab=dashboard` falls back to Deviations. The three case screens are the one cached case list cut by type, each raising only its own types (a non-conformance and a complaint are preset, not asked). The rail shows open counts beside each (`fetchQualityCounts`, keyed under the deviations prefix so any Quality save refreshes them). A **complaint** is a fourth case type, numbered `CMP-2026-001`, asking *Received via* (required) and *Injury or adverse reaction reported* (tri-state, required at close). The case record's tabs are now Initiation · Risk · Investigation · CAPA · Close out · Aging: the **Containment** tab is folded into Initiation as its last block (immediate action date and immediate action taken, optional when raising, required at close-out — `ContainmentTab` is kept, unwired), the Risk tab scores Likelihood, Severity of impact and Probability of detection 1–5 with each point described, and shows the calculation (score = severity × likelihood × detection; 20+ Medium, 60+ High, severity 5 always High — `0058`); the Investigation tab adds an optional **Root cause category** (Man, Method, Material, Machine, Environment); and the Corrective and Preventive tabs are replaced by one **CAPA** tab offering three paths: **Resolve directly** (a correction — stored as the reason no CAPA is needed), **Initiate CAPA** (one form, `initiate_capa()` / `0059`: title, reason, owner, target date, root cause + method, CA and PA rows, effectiveness criteria + verify-by), **Link to existing CAPA** (an open one with the same root cause — one CAPA can cover several cases). Once linked, the CAPA is worked right there. **Since `0073` a CAPA runs CAPA plan → Do → Effectiveness check → Closed** on its own page (`/deviations/capa/[capaId]`, `CapaPage`, step cards `capa-plan-step` / `capa-do-step` / `capa-check-step`, `CapaStepper`): the plan (actions with what / who / due, how we will know it worked, check on a date — quick +1/+3/+6 months from the last due date — or when the actions are done with why, who checks) is saved as written and then **approved** (comment optional; supervisor and up, the owner may approve their own for now, warned); actions are then **marked done** with evidence and an optional linked record (CC / training), and more can be added; once all are done the **effectiveness check** is signed — Worked closes it, Didn't work sends it back to Do for a new action and a re-check; an early dated check asks why; every check is listed. **History** (collapsible, `capa_audit`) records every change, marked *Changed / Added after plan approval*. The register lists views All open · Plans to approve · Actions in progress · Checks due · Closed · All, with Step (dots) and Next columns and the first-check success rate; a row opens the CAPA page. The case's Actions tab: Resolve directly, **Create a new CAPA** (title, owner, why, optional first action, root cause shown → opens the CAPA page) or Link to an open CAPA; once linked, a card with the stepper, what is next and Open CAPA (Unlink only in the plan step). `CapaPanel` and `CapaDetailDialog` are gone. Before `0073`: a **CAPA** (`CAPA-2026-001`, `src/components/factory/capa/`, `capa-queries.ts`) has an owner, a verify-by date, effectiveness criteria, and CA / PA **actions** carrying the old tabs' fields under the same labels — owner, target date, actual closed date, closed by, description; an action is closed with a date and a name and is then frozen. Once every action is closed it is **verified as effective** (verification + verified by) and frozen. The close-out now also needs the CAPA decision: Yes → the linked CAPA effective; No → the reason. Existing inline CA/PA was converted into CAPA records by `0057`. The old "Linked CAPA" picker to Issues & CAPA is no longer drawn (`action_id` is kept). Everything below about the case record still holds. Originally **Deviations & NCRs** — the QA register, under **Accountability**, supervisor and up (`can_review_factory()` for raising and closing, `0044`). Three types and the type is part of the number: planned and unplanned deviations are `DEV-2026-004`, an NCR is `NCR-2026-001`. Specification, what happened, impact, raised by, QA reviewer, an optional linked CAPA; an NCR also takes a **disposition** (use as is / rework / reject / quarantine). One gate, **Open → Closed**, paid for with a QA name and a closing note — and for an NCR a final disposition that is not quarantine, because quarantine is a hold, not an outcome. A closed record is frozen. **Quarantine reaches past the module**: an open quarantine NCR puts the batch's card On hold (Quality), the shift log **refuses preparatory and production entries** against it until the NCR is closed, downtime is still accepted but never releases the hold, and after closing the next preparatory or production entry releases it — a rejected batch simply stays held. The board card, the job dialog, the log form and the grid name the NCR from `quarantine_no` on the board's view. The batch is typed and resolved to a product by the database trigger. Components in `src/components/factory/deviations/`, queries in `deviation-queries.ts`. **Raising a deviation is a page** (`/deviations/new`, `RaiseDeviationPage`, since `0062`), not the dialog — NCRs and complaints keep the dialog. It asks what the reference QMS (qms-v4) asks, two columns: title; planned or unplanned (a toggle, Unplanned first); when it happened (date and time, now by default); department (Configuration → Departments); location; detected at; batches (several, comma-separated, each resolved to product, code and customer as typed); what happened; immediate action (required, with a *None needed* link); put the batch on hold? (No / Yes, required — Yes places a **batch hold** on every named batch, which needs each in the register); equipment; reference document / SOP; procedure name; and optional **attachments** (`PendingAttachments` — held in the browser, up to 10, and uploaded to the case's Details straight after the insert, since a file is stored under the case's id; one that fails is named in a toast and does not undo the raise). Priority, due date, owner and supervisor are not asked there; owner, class and due date come from the initial assessment. **Once raised, the answers are locked**: the case's Details tab (and the case dialog) shows them read-only (`DeviationInitiationFacts`) — only attachments can still be added there. Raising opens the new case (`?case=<id>` on the register). A case's batches all count on the Batch record. **A deviation opens as a page** (`/deviations/[caseId]`, `DeviationCasePage`, since `0063`) rather than the case dialog (NCRs and complaints, and the Batch record, still use the dialog): breadcrumb; title and meta line; five facts — Stage (derived by `devStage`: Awaiting assessment → Investigating → Actions → Awaiting CAPA → Ready to close → Closed), Class, Risk score, Assess by (raise + 2 days) / Due, Owner; the hold banner; a **Next:** bar with a jump to its tab; and tabs **Details · Assessment · Investigation · Actions · Close-out · History** (`?section=`), each ticked when done. Details is the raise form filled in, read-only, then **Batch holds** (`BatchHolds`, `hold-queries.ts`, `0069`): one row per held batch — Batch · Status · Placed · Decision — and, for supervisor and up on an open case, **Release** (why it is safe while the case stays open; needs the product impact assessment unless no investigation is required; warns when the investigation is not approved) or **Reject** (reason), and **Put a batch on hold** (typed batch numbers). A released batch's card comes off hold with its next preparatory or production entry; a **rejected batch stays blocked in the shift log for good**. The Investigation tab offers to hold the other batches affected. The case cannot close (nor take its first close-out signature) while a batch is on hold, and the banner names the batches on hold or released. Header "Assess by" is raise + *Initial assessment — days* and the due date set on signing is raise + the class's *days to close*, both from **Configuration → Quality settings** (`/admin?tab=quality`, `QualitySettingsForm`, `quality-settings-queries.ts`, `0071`: Critical 15 / Major 30 / Minor 60 / assess 2 by default, manager and up, every change in a settings change log; a change applies to cases assessed after it). **Extend due date** (case page, beside Print report; `due-extensions.tsx`, `extension-queries.ts`, `0072`): an open, assessed case asks for a later date with a reason — a banner shows it waiting — and QA approves (the due date moves, the header shows *(ext.)*) or rejects it with a reason on History → *Due date extensions*, between the timeline and the audit trail; the requester may approve their own for now (warned). A deviation also has a **Print report** (`/deviations/[caseId]/print`, `DeviationReport`): the header and every section read-only, one after another, with *Print or save as PDF* (A4 portrait) and a printed-by footer. **Assessment** is the reference QMS's initial assessment (`AssessmentTab`): category, case owner, investigation required (+ why not); red flags (any → severity 5, Critical); the risk as qms-v5 scores it (`0068`): **severity 1–5** (the outcome for the product — contained before release is 3, reached the market is 4) and **likelihood 1–3** (first known occurrence / happened before / recurring), backed by **evidence** — previous cases of the same problem picked from a search over every case in the factory, or *Searched records — no previous case found*, or cases kept outside FactoryOS — the picked cases suggest the likelihood (4+, or 2 within 90 days → 3; any → 2) and picking raises it; lower than suggested needs the rationale; **detection is not asked**; the class is read from a **table** (severity 1–2 / 3–4 / 5 × likelihood: Minor Minor Major · Major Major Critical · Critical ×3; any red flag Critical), follows the table until QA picks another (+ why when it differs); rationale required for Major / Critical; recurring (3) → a CAPA is required (signing sets *CAPA needed*, the Actions tab says so). The header and register show the risk as `S3 · L2`; assessments signed before `0068` keep their 1–125 score and are shown the old way. The Batch record opens a deviation on its page, not the old dialog; **Save draft** or **Sign initial assessment**, which sets the due date (15 / 30 / 60 days) and is then read-only. **Investigation** (`DevInvestigationTab`, `0064`) is the reference QMS's: Impact (other batches affected, resolved as typed; product impact assessment), What was reviewed (`0070`: tick each item looked at — batch record, equipment history, previous similar cases, environmental data, raw material or packaging changes, training records, other batches, or **Add other item** — and write **what it showed** beside it; submitting needs a finding for every ticked item), Root cause (findings; 6M categories — ticking Man (people) asks whether the procedure was clear, the person trained, and whether the process could have prevented it; root cause; conclusion; method), attachments; **Save draft**, then **Submit for approval** (after the assessment is signed), then QA's **Approve investigation** or **Send back** with a reason. Submitted or approved it is the same form, disabled. Not required (from the assessment) shows the reason instead. **Attachments** (`CaseAttachments`, `attachment-queries.ts`) sit on Details and Investigation: browser → Supabase Storage (`case-files`) directly, 10 MB each, 10 per pick, PDF / images / Word / Excel / CSV / text, opened by a 60-second signed URL, removable by the uploader while the case is open. Actions is the CAPA tab with three paths (Resolve directly, Initiate CAPA, Link to existing CAPA). **Close-out** (`DevCloseOutTab`, `0065`): a **Before closing** checklist (`devChecklist` — assessment signed; investigation approved or not required; immediate action; CAPA decision; the CAPA verified effective, when there is one; no unresolved risk; summary and reason), then any unresolved product or patient risk (Yes refuses the close), close-out summary and residual risk, reason (Resolved / No action required / Duplicate) and *Duplicate of* or an optional cost of quality; **Save draft** and **Sign close-out**, enabled when the list is complete. A Critical case needs two signatures: the first leaves it Awaiting 2nd signature with the close-out fixed, the second closes it (same person is warned, not blocked, for now). **History** (`CaseHistory`, `audit-queries.ts`, `0066`): the timeline (raised, assessment, investigation, actions agreed, CAPA effective, closed) and the **audit trail** — every change the database recorded, newest first, as qms-v5's table — **When · Who · What · From · To · Reason**, one row per change; lists are told item by item ("Reviewed item ticked / unticked", "Finding: Batch record", "Likelihood evidence: previous case added"), steps and signatures as "Signed: Initial assessment approved", "Signed: Batch released" (From On hold, To the batch, with the reason); long values clipped to two lines with Show more and the full text on hover — filtered by All / Changes / Signatures & steps / Files. Written by triggers, so it covers every screen that edits a case, the dialog included.
- **Floor status** (`/factory/[slug]/floor`) — every room as a card, from the **latest shift-log entry it filed on one calendar day**. First item under **Quality & maintenance**, everyone, read-only. The board is a day, not a shift: at midnight every card goes back to idle (the day follows `useRenderClock(todayKey)` until a past day is picked). A card is teal / running when its latest entry is preparatory or production, amber / stopped when it is downtime (then it also shows the stage the room was on before the stop), grey / idle when nothing was logged. Progress is the stage's `accumulated_qty` when viewing today, so it matches the dialog, and the entry's `accumulative` from `shift_log_entries_expanded` on a past day, so it reads as of then. Open-issue counts come from unclosed `actions` matched on `batch_no`. All / Active / Idle filter and Refresh sit top right; a live board refetches every minute. Clicking a card opens two tabs: **Batch progress** (the batch's whole plan, stage by stage, with the room's current stage ringed) and **Room schedule** (the plan's `unit_id` / `planned_date` read against a day that can be stepped backwards and forwards). Derivation in `floor-queries.ts` (`buildFloorRooms`, `roomSchedule`); entries are keyed under the shift log's prefix, so filing an entry refreshes the board, and jobs are read under `floorKeys.jobs`, not `pipelineKeys.all`, so the board page's promotion is never skipped. Components in `src/components/factory/floor/`.
- **Batch record** (`/factory/[slug]/batch`) — one batch, everything that happened to it, read-only. A debounced search over the catalogue (batch number, product, code or work order) opens a masthead and four tabs: **Activity** (every shift-log entry filed against the batch — named Activity, not Production, because a room's downtime and its mixing steps belong to the record as much as its production runs), **Issues** (the `actions` raised against it) and **Maintenance** (the requests naming it) and **Deviations** (the deviations and NCRs naming it, closable from `DeviationDetailDialog` as on their own screen). Counts sit on the tabs, so "were there any issues?" is answered without opening one. Cards open the modules' own detail dialogs — `ActionDetailDialog`, `MaintenanceDetailDialog` — so an issue worked from here is worked exactly as it is from Issues & CAPAs; the shift-log entry gets a read-only `BatchEntryDialog`, because corrections belong where the work is (the feed and the shift report both stamp who amended and why). Issues, maintenance and deviations are **matched on `batch_no`, not `product_id`**: both keep the number that was typed even when it matched no product, and matching on the id would drop the records most worth finding. Everyone may read it; it writes nothing.
